# Proposal: MCIB Specification v1.4 — Accelerator Time-Sharing

**Status:** Proposal. Not normative. Enters trial with Campaign #3
(`campaigns/campaign-3-accelerator-time-sharing.md`) and is adopted, revised
or withdrawn on that campaign's evidence, per the specification's change
policy ("revisions only when a campaign demonstrates necessity").
**Base:** MCIB Specification v1.3.
**Author:** Lei Zhou

---

## Why a revision

v1.3 assumes the victim executes on a CPU (F1, F2) or waits on a device
request (F3), and that an accelerator appears only as an *aggressor*: a DMA
master whose traffic slows CPU work (`hw.dma.master_traffic`).

Campaign #3 needs the converse: the **victim's own work executes on the
accelerator**, and the interference happens *inside* the accelerator — in its
engine time-sharing and firmware scheduler — where no CPU-side layer applies.
Two gaps follow:

1. No victim family measures an accelerator job against a deadline.
2. No layer covers time that accrues inside a shared accelerator. By the
   layer-assignment rule ("the layer where the time accrues"), assigning it
   to `hw.dma`, `hw.core` or `sw.runtime` would be wrong.

Layers are a closed set revised only by spec revision, so (2) cannot be
solved in the mechanism registry.

## Change 1 — Victim family F4: accelerator job latency

**F4 — Accelerator job latency.** Latency of a unit of victim work executed
on a shared accelerator (GPU, NPU), measured against a declared deadline.

```
T0  submit      the victim's job is handed to the accelerator driver
                (host-observed at the driver boundary; for a guest victim,
                 the guest-observed submit, with the boundary transport
                 declared per §1.3)
TS  start       the accelerator begins executing the job (where observable)
T1  complete    the job's completion is signalled (fence or timestamp)
TD  deadline    the job's declared deadline; for display work, the scanout
                vblank the frame is due for
```

Reported metrics:

| ID | Definition |
|---|---|
| `AJLat` | Job completion latency, T1 − T0 |
| `AJPre` | Preemption latency, TS − T0, for victim jobs submitted while an aggressor job is executing. Reported only where TS is observable; otherwise declared unobservable |
| `AJMiss` | Deadline-miss ratio: fraction of victim jobs with T1 > TD (display: frame not ready for its vblank) |

Constraints `[NORMATIVE]`:

- **Clock domain:** accelerator timestamps and CPU timestamps are different
  domains on most platforms. The §1.1 clock-domain constraint applies to any
  interval spanning both; the calibration method between them is declared.
- **Deadline declaration:** TD is a configuration parameter of the run,
  stated before measurement, never inferred from the data.
- **Distribution reporting** follows §1.4, with `AJMiss` added. Floor/tail
  decomposition separates the victim's isolated execution time (floor) from
  contention-driven delay (tail).

## Change 2 — New layer `hw.accel`

| Layer | Covers |
|---|---|
| `hw.accel` | Accelerator-internal execution resources: engine and queue time-sharing, firmware or hardware job scheduling, preemption granularity, accelerator-local caches and memory |

Boundary with existing layers:

- `hw.dma` remains the layer for an accelerator's traffic contending on a
  path *shared with the CPU side* (interconnect, memory controller as seen
  by a CPU victim).
- `hw.accel` is the layer where an accelerator-executed victim waits for
  the accelerator itself.
- A virtualisation backend that loses or rewrites the victim's scheduling
  priority accrues time in `sw.hypervisor`, not `hw.accel`; the effect is
  observed in `hw.accel`, the cause is attributed where it arises.

## Change 3 — Mechanism registry (`taxonomy_version: 2`)

Seed entries, registered as expected instances (as the v1 seeds were), not
findings:

| Identifier | Layer | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `hw.accel.engine_timeslice` | `hw.accel` | Victim job waits for an engine or queue slot held by an aggressor's work under time-sliced scheduling. |
| `hw.accel.nonpreempt_job` | `hw.accel` | Victim job is blocked by an executing aggressor job until that job completes or reaches a preemption point. |

Proposed as an extension, not registered (rule of two):
`sw.hypervisor.x-priority_not_propagated` — guest-assigned job priority is
not conveyed to the host accelerator scheduler by the virtualisation
backend.

## Change 4 — Schema

- `bottleneck_class: compute-bound` additionally maps to `hw.accel` (an
  aggressor saturating accelerator engine time). No new value.
- New fields, required on F4 runs: `deadline_us`, `miss_ratio`,
  `accel_id` (accelerator and driver identity, auto-populated).
- New field, optional on F4 runs: `ajpre_observable` (boolean) with
  `ajpre_p50_us` / `ajpre_p99_us` / `ajpre_max_us` when true.
- `isolation_config` declares the accelerator scheduling controls applied
  (priority level, queue/group placement, partition identifier).

## Change 5 — Verdict semantics for accelerators

| Verdict | Accelerator meaning |
|---|---|
| `tune` | Driver- or backend-level configuration removes the outlier class: scheduling priority, queue placement, priority propagation through the virtualisation backend |
| `partition` | A hardware accelerator partition or isolated lane bounds it |
| `ceiling` | Preemption granularity bounds it: the residual tracks aggressor job length and no available control moves it; the design must bound aggressor job length or move the critical work off the accelerator |

## Change 6 — §5 acceleration path declaration

A port measuring F4 additionally declares which scheduling controls the
open driver exposes for each accelerator (priority levels, preemption
support and level, partitioning), and which of them its instrumentation can
observe.

## Migration

- F1–F3 datasets are unaffected. Datasets that declare
  `taxonomy_version: 1` remain conforming.
- F4 datasets declare `taxonomy_version: 2` and `schema_version` carrying
  the Change 4 fields.

## Open questions

1. Should F4 be a new family or an F3 sub-family? F3's paired-path method
   (virtualised vs native) is useful for F4 too, but F3's victim waits on a
   device rather than executing on it.
2. Is `AJPre` observable on any open GPU stack without vendor tooling? If
   no platform exposes TS, `AJPre` becomes a differential estimate and its
   definition should say so.
3. Does `hw.accel` need sub-structure (engine vs accelerator-local memory),
   or is the mechanism registry enough?
